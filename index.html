Dual-Stage Alignment for Robust and Instruction-Guided Voice Design via Decoupled GRPO

1University of Science and Technology of China, Hefei, China

ICASSP 2027 · Chinese voice-design examples

Abstract

Natural-language voice design requires both faithful semantic control and stable speech synthesis, yet existing systems often exhibit semantic drift and synthesis instability. A key limitation is that joint instruction–acoustic conditioning requires scarce annotated instruction–voice pairs, which also prevents effective reuse of large-scale acoustic pre-training. Stable-Timbre addresses this limitation by generating continuous speaker x-vectors from design instructions with a flow-matching policy and passing them to Stable-ALM within a unified x-vector–FSQ framework. CCC first strengthens core-attribute control. Dual-stage GRPO then improves semantic faithfulness with CLAP rewards and speech intelligibility with post-synthesis WER feedback. Ablations verify this progression: CCC improves core-attribute control, CLAP alignment strengthens semantic faithfulness, and synthesis-feedback alignment improves synthesis intelligibility. On the Chinese split of the InstructTTSEval benchmark (ITTS-ZH), Stable-Timbre obtains WER 1.53% versus 3.94% for our controlled instruction-conditioned DeepDub baseline, with an Acoustic-Parameter Specification (APS) score of 89.39 and a naturalness mean opinion score of 4.35.

M is the timbre policy initialized after CCC; c is the voice-design instruction and x the synthesis text. The acoustic foundation F, CLAP, and ASR remain frozen throughout both stages.
